<|det|>text [43, 640, 197, 647]<|/det|>Coby White is not a dunkler.
<|det|>text [42, 644, 213, 687]<|/det|>Before Monday's 114-110 win over the San Antonio Spurs, the Bulls guard had attempted only two dunks this season. He dunked only 14 times last season. It's just not a part of his bag that White likes to dig into often.
<|det|>image_footnote [222, 701, 548, 714]<|/det|>Bulls guard Zach LaVine slides on the court during the fourth quarter against the Spurs on Monday night.
<|det|>text [43, 687, 213, 712]<|/det|>Maybe, he joked after the win, he would try it more if he had the athleticism of Zach LaVine or the wingspan of Patrick Williams.
<|det|>text [42, 712, 213, 741]<|/det|>But in the final 30 seconds, with a one-point lead over the Spurs, White didn’t have another choice. So he put his head down and charged to the rim. And if the
<|det|>text [43, 741, 212, 755]<|/det|>tallest man in the NBA was in his way? Well, all the better.
<|det|>text [43, 755, 213, 808]<|/det|>Victor Wembanyama already had blocked three of White's shots before the final minute, but that didn't slow White's approach. It couldn't. After falling behind by 19 points in the first half, the Bulls couldn't collapse at the rim just because the 7-foot-3 Wembanyama was waiting.
<|det|>text [43, 808, 213, 834]<|/det|>In the locker room at halftime, the entire Bulls roster shared the same sentiment: We have to do something.
<|det|>text [43, 834, 213, 865]<|/det|>For White, that something meant attacking the rim. Even if he got blocked. And in the final minute, that fearlessness paid off twofold.
<|det|>text [43, 865, 213, 927]<|/det|>White slung an up-and-under shot that arched all the way over Wembanyama's 8-foot wingspan and into the basket to give the Bulls the lead with 47 seconds remaining. And 32 seconds later, White took an even more aggressive approach, getting Jeremy Sochan to bite on a pump fake and launching himself skyward.
<|det|>text [43, 927, 213, 970]<|/det|>Wembanyama rotated over a second late. That didn’t matter either. For White, the poster-perfect shot — his arm cocked back, Wembanyama’s body crashing into his torso, the ball already searing downward toward the
<|det|>text [222, 718, 392, 731]<|/det|>rim – was the only option to clinch the game.
<|det|>text [222, 731, 392, 788]<|/det|>“I just told myself the next time I was going to go, I was going to try to dunk it,” White said. “I knew when I was driving, if I was going to lay it in, finger-roll it, anything, he was going to block it. So I just was like — I’ve got to dunk it. I just wanted to make sure that I got the basket.”

<|det|>text [222, 805, 392, 857]<|/det|>1. Wemby vs. Vooch: The toughest matchup of the game was between Wembanyama and Bulls center Nikola Vučević. From the start, it was a challenge for Vučević — Wembanyama blocked him twice in the first four minutes, enforcing his will under and around the rim.

The veteran extended his consistent scoring, tallying 24 points despite an off night (2-for-9) behind the 3-point arc. But Wembanyama proved equally vexing on the other end, making four 3-pointers to score 23 points.
<|det|>text [222, 903, 392, 969]<|/det|>In the final two minutes of the second quarter, Vucevic chased Wembanyama out to the 3-point line, doggedly latched onto the Spurs center's hip until the shot clock expired. <|det|>text [403, 718, 504, 725]<|/det|>second of the night.

<|det|>text [402, 761, 572, 807]<|/det|>Wembanyama closed the game with an identical play in the final 10 seconds. This time, the shot rattled out. And for Vucevic, that was victory enough — forcing Wembanyama into his worst shot for the game-winner.
<|det|>text [402, 812, 572, 878]<|/det|>2. Zach LaVine brought the highlights: It’s hard to fly under the radar while consistently putting up 30 or more points, but LaVine is achieving just that. <|det|>text [402, 878, 572, 945]<|/det|>His scoring was vital on a cold night from 3-point range. The Bulls went 13-for-39 behind the arc — and LaVine and White combined for six of those makes. <|det|>text [597, 315, 772, 388]<|/det|>The Bulls have not sent a player to the All-Star game since 2023, when DeMar DeRozan was the team's sole representative in the game. <|det|>text [597, 388, 772, 467]<|/det|>This season, veterans LaVine and Nikola Vučević might be flying under the radar as potential selections for the All-Star game. <|det|>text [597, 466, 772, 550]<|/det|>All-Star starters will be announced on Jan. 23 and the reserve team will be announced on Jan. 30. Three of those teams will consist of All-Stars while the fourth will be the winning team from the Rising Stars tournament for rookie, second-year and G League players.

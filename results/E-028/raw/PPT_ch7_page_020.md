<|det|>header [678, 20, 967, 58]<|/det|>7.2 Matrix Multiplication
<|det|>text [50, 109, 894, 214]<|/det|>Our examples show that in matrix products the order of factors must always be observed very carefully.
<|det|>text [51, 216, 894, 321]<|/det|>Otherwise matrix multiplication satisfies rules similar to those for numbers, namely.
<|det|>text [124, 344, 540, 400]<|/det|>(a) \((k\mathbf{A})\mathbf{B} = k(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}) = \mathbf{A}(k\mathbf{B})\)
<|det|>text [576, 346, 862, 393]<|/det|>written kAB or AkB
<|det|>text [58, 416, 427, 478]<|/det|>(b) \(\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{BC}) = (\mathbf{AB})\mathbf{C}\)
<|det|>text [679, 416, 866, 460]<|/det|>written ABC
<|det|>text [124, 481, 456, 533]<|/det|>(c) \((\mathbf{A} + \mathbf{B})\mathbf{C} = \mathbf{A}\mathbf{C} + \mathbf{B}\mathbf{C}\)
<|det|>text [120, 547, 456, 599]<|/det|>(d) \(\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{A} + \mathbf{B}) = \mathbf{C}\mathbf{A} + \mathbf{C}\mathbf{B}\)
<|det|>text [53, 643, 910, 848]<|/det|>provided A, B, and C are such that the expressions on the left are defined; here, k is any scalar. (2b) is called the associative law. (2c) and (2d) are called the distributive laws.

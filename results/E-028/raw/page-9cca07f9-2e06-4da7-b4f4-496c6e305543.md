<|det|>text [141, 494, 854, 533]<|/det|>Lemma 22 Assume Assumption 1. Let \(\mathbf{z}_k\sim \rho_k^{z,\eta}\) for any \(\rho_k^{z,\eta}\in \mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{2dN})\), and let \(\mathbf{z}_{k + 1}\sim \rho_{k + 1}^{z,\eta}\) be one step of the finite-particle algorithm (20) with step size \(0 < \eta \leq \frac{\alpha}{64L^2}\). Then:
<|det|>equation [228, 542, 769, 575]<|/det|>\[
W _ {2} (\rho_ {k + 1} ^ {\mathbf {z}, \eta}, \bar {\nu} ^ {\mathbf {Z}}) ^ {2} \leq e ^ {- \frac {3}{2} \alpha \eta} W _ {2} (\rho_ {k} ^ {\mathbf {z}, \eta}, \bar {\nu} ^ {\mathbf {Z}}) ^ {2} + \frac {8 \eta L ^ {2}}{\alpha} \left(\mathsf {V a r} _ {\bar {\nu} ^ {Z}} (\bar {Z}) + 6 4 \tau \eta d N\right).
\]
<|det|>text [142, 586, 855, 621]<|/det|>Proof We consider a continuous-time interpolation of one step of the algorithm (20) as follows. Let  \( Z_{0} \sim \rho_{0}^{Z} \)  where we define  \( \rho_{0}^{Z} = \rho_{k}^{z,\eta} \) . We define  \( (\mathbf{Z}_{t})_{0 \leq t \leq \eta} \)  where  \( Z_{t} \sim \rho_{t}^{Z} \)  evolves following:
<|det|>equation [385, 630, 855, 651]<|/det|>\[
d \mathbf {Z} _ {t} = b ^ {\mathbf {Z}} (\mathbf {Z} _ {0}) d t + \sqrt {2 \tau} d W _ {t} ^ {\mathbf {Z}} \tag {49}
\]
<|det|>text [141, 663, 854, 734]<|/det|>where  \( (W_{t}^{\mathbf{Z}})_{t\geq0} \)  is the standard Brownian motion in  \( R^{2dN} \)  which is independent of  \( Z_{0} \) . Then we notice that the distribution of  \( Z_{\eta}\sim\rho_{\eta}^{Z} \)  of the process (49) is equal to the distribution of the next iterate  \( x_{k+1}\sim\rho_{k+1}^{z,\eta} \)  along the algorithm (20), i.e.,  \( \rho_{k+1}^{z,\eta}=\rho_{\eta}^{Z} \) . This is because the solution to the process (49) at time  \( t=\eta \)  is:
<|det|>equation [277, 745, 720, 767]<|/det|>\[
\mathbf {Z} _ {t} = \mathbf {Z} _ {0} + t b ^ {\mathbf {Z}} (\mathbf {Z} _ {0}) + \sqrt {2 \tau} W _ {t} ^ {\mathbf {Z}} \stackrel {d} {=} \mathbf {Z} _ {0} + t b ^ {\mathbf {Z}} (\mathbf {Z} _ {0}) + \sqrt {2 \tau t} \zeta
\]
<|det|>text [141, 782, 854, 823]<|/det|>where \(\zeta \sim \mathcal{N}(0,I)\) is an independent Gaussian random variable in \(\mathbb{R}^{2dN}\), and \(\stackrel{d}{=}\) means equality in distribution. This is the same update of the algorithm (20), so indeed \(\mathbf{Z}_{\eta} \stackrel{d}{=} \mathbf{z}_{k+1}\), so \(\rho_{\eta}^{\mathbf{Z}} = \rho_{k+1}^{\mathbf{z},\eta}\).

<|det|>equation [386, 867, 855, 888]<|/det|>\[

<|det|>text [78, 121, 919, 153]<|/det|>For consistency, the time derivative of the constraints of (10) must vanish and hence they must have vanishing Poisson bracket with \( H \). Using the fundamental Poisson brackets
<|det|>equation [124, 161, 918, 181]<|/det|>\[
\left[ U (x), \Pi^ {U} (y) \right] = \delta (x - y), \tag {13}
\]
<|det|>text [78, 188, 673, 205]<|/det|>etc., we find that the primary constraints of (10) imply the secondary constraints
<|det|>equation [124, 212, 918, 233]<|/det|>\[
(\Sigma , \Sigma_ {i}) = \left(- \partial_ {k} \Pi_ {k} ^ {V}, \varepsilon^ {i j k} \partial_ {j} \left(\Pi_ {k} ^ {B} - m V _ {k}\right) - \mu^ {2} B _ {i}\right). \tag {14}
\]
<|det|>text [78, 240, 918, 288]<|/det|>If \(\mu^2 = 0\) (the Cremmer–Scherk model Lagrangian [1]), the constraints of (14) would become reducible as then \(\partial_i\Sigma_i = 0\) and only the transverse portions of \(\Sigma_i\) are constraints. Furthermore, with \(\mu^2\neq 0\), the requirement \(\dot{\Sigma}_i = 0\) leads to a tertiary constraint
<|det|>equation [124, 296, 918, 315]<|/det|>\[
T _ {k} \equiv \mu^ {2} \Pi_ {k} ^ {B} = 0 \tag {15}
\]
<|det|>text [78, 322, 486, 338]<|/det|>with \(\Sigma_{i}\) and \(T_{k}\) constituting second class constraints as
<|det|>equation [124, 347, 918, 367]<|/det|>\[
\left[ T _ {k} (x), \Sigma_ {i} (y) \right] = \mu^ {4} \delta_ {i k} \delta (x - y). \tag {16}
\]
<|det|>text [77, 373, 918, 469]<|/det|>All other constraints are first class and no further constraints need to be imposed for consistency. There are consequently five first class constraints \((\Phi^U, \Phi_A^k\) and \(\Sigma)\) and six second class constraints \((\Sigma_i\) and \(T_k)\). The constraints \(\Phi^U\) and \(\Sigma\) correspond to the usual gauge transformations \(\delta W_0 = \partial_0\Omega\), \(\delta W_i = \partial_i\Omega\) associated with a gauge field \(W_\mu\), while \(\Phi_A^k\) is associated with the fact that in (12) \(A_k\) acts merely as a Lagrange multiplier (i.e., it is not dynamical) and hence its value is completely arbitrary. Suitable gauge conditions associated with the first class constraints are
<|det|>equation [124, 479, 918, 500]<|/det|>\[
\left(\gamma^ {U}, \gamma_ {A} ^ {K}, \gamma^ {V}\right) = (U, A _ {k}, \partial_ {k} V _ {k}) = 0. \tag {17}
\]
<|det|>text [78, 506, 742, 522]<|/det|>From (10), (14), (15) and (17) it is evident that the only dynamical degrees of freedom are
<|det|>equation [124, 529, 918, 551]<|/det|>\[
V _ {i} ^ {T} \equiv \left(\delta_ {i j} - \partial_ {i} \partial_ {j} / \partial^ {2}\right) V _ {j}. \tag {18}
\]
<|det|>text [78, 556, 918, 588]<|/det|>We can verify this directly by explicitly eliminating the non-physical degrees of freedom in (4). First, one decomposes \( V_{k} \), \( A_{k} \) and \( B_{k} \) into transverse \( (T) \) and longitudinal \( (L) \) parts where
<|det|>equation [123, 596, 918, 615]<|/det|>\[
\nabla \times \mathbf {V} ^ {L} \equiv 0 \equiv \nabla \cdot \mathbf {V} ^ {T}, \tag {19}
\]
<|det|>text [78, 623, 247, 638]<|/det|>etc., (4) now becomes
<|det|>equation [122, 644, 918, 692]<|/det|>\[
\begin{array}{l} 2 L = \left(\dot {\mathbf {B}} ^ {L}\right) ^ {2} - \left(\nabla \cdot \mathbf {B} ^ {L}\right) ^ {2} + \left[ \dot {\mathbf {B}} ^ {T} - \nabla \times \mathbf {A} ^ {T} \right] ^ {2} + \left(\dot {\mathbf {V}} ^ {T}\right) ^ {2} - \left(\nabla \times \mathbf {V} ^ {T}\right) ^ {2} + \left[ \dot {\mathbf {V}} ^ {L} - \nabla U \right] ^ {2} \\ + 2 m \left[ \mathbf {V} ^ {T} \cdot \left(\nabla \times \mathbf {A} ^ {T}\right) + \mathbf {B} ^ {L} \cdot \dot {\mathbf {V}} ^ {L} + \mathbf {B} ^ {T} \cdot \dot {\mathbf {V}} ^ {T} - \mathbf {B} ^ {L} \cdot \nabla U \right] + 2 \mu^ {2} \left[ \mathbf {A} ^ {T} \cdot \mathbf {B} ^ {T} + \mathbf {A} ^ {L} \cdot \mathbf {B} ^ {L} \right]. \tag {20} \\ \end{array}
\]
<|det|>text [78, 698, 548, 714]<|/det|>The equations of motion for \(\mathbf{A}^L\) and \(U\), respectively, imply that
<|det|>equation [122, 721, 918, 740]<|/det|>\[
\mathbf {B} ^ {L} = 0 = \dot {\mathbf {V}} ^ {L} - \nabla U, \tag {21}
\]
<|det|>text [78, 748, 202, 764]<|/det|>reducing (20) to
<|det|>equation [122, 771, 918, 793]<|/det|>\[
2 L = \left(\dot {\mathbf {V}} ^ {T}\right) ^ {2} - \left(\nabla \times \mathbf {V} ^ {T}\right) ^ {2} + \left[ \dot {\mathbf {B}} ^ {T} - \nabla \times \mathbf {A} ^ {T} \right] ^ {2} + 2 m \mathbf {V} ^ {T} \cdot \left(\nabla \times \mathbf {A} ^ {T}\right) + 2 m \dot {\mathbf {B}} ^ {T} \cdot \dot {\mathbf {V}} ^ {T} + 2 \mu^ {2} \mathbf {A} ^ {T} \cdot \mathbf {B} ^ {T}. \tag {22}
\]
<|det|>text [78, 800, 125, 813]<|/det|>Since
<|det|>equation [122, 822, 918, 843]<|/det|>\[
\mathbf {A} ^ {T} \cdot \mathbf {B} ^ {T} = - (\nabla \times \mathbf {A} ^ {T}) \cdot (\nabla^ {2}) ^ {- 1} (\nabla \times \mathbf {B} ^ {T}), \tag {23}
\]
<|det|>text [78, 851, 419, 867]<|/det|>we can eliminate \(\nabla \times \mathbf{A}^T\) from (22) to obtain
<|det|>equation [122, 875, 918, 897]<|/det|>\[
\nabla \times \mathbf {A} ^ {T} = \dot {\mathbf {B}} ^ {T} - m \nabla^ {T} + \mu^ {2} (\nabla^ {2}) ^ {- 1} (\nabla \times \mathbf {B} ^ {T}). \tag {24}
\]

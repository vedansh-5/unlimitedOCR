<|det|>text [200, 180, 794, 210]<|/det|>In what follows, we will write \(\mathrm{Hom}_G\) instead of \(\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}G}\) and \(\otimes_G\) instead of \(\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}G}\). Because of the special role of the trivial \(G\)-module \(\mathbb{Z}\), the augmentation
<|det|>equation [450, 216, 543, 232]<|/det|>\[
\varepsilon \colon \mathbb {Z} G \to \mathbb {Z},
\]
<|det|>text [201, 238, 275, 253]<|/det|>defined by
<|det|>equation [413, 252, 580, 285]<|/det|>\[
\varepsilon \colon \sum_ {x \in G} m _ {x} x \mapsto \sum_ {x \in G} m _ {x},
\]
<|det|>text [200, 287, 794, 332]<|/det|>is important. Recall that we have seen, in Exercise 8.37 on page 573, that \(\varepsilon\) is a surjective ring homomorphism, and so its kernel, \(\mathcal{G}\), is a two-sided ideal in \(\mathbb{Z}G\), called the augmentation ideal. Thus, there is an exact sequence
<|det|>equation [400, 339, 592, 356]<|/det|>\[
0 \to \mathcal {G} \to \mathbb {Z} G \xrightarrow {\varepsilon} \mathbb {Z} \to 0.
\]
<|det|>text [200, 368, 794, 398]<|/det|>Proposition 10.103. Let \( G \) be a group with augmentation ideal \( \mathcal{G} \). As an abelian group, \( \mathcal{G} \) is free abelian with basis \( G - 1 = \{x - 1 : x \in G, x \neq 1\} \).
<|det|>text [201, 403, 794, 433]<|/det|>Proof. An element \( u = \sum_{x} m_{x} x \in \mathbb{Z}G \) lies in \( \ker \varepsilon = \mathcal{G} \) if and only if \( \sum_{x} m_{x} = 0 \). Therefore, if \( u \in \mathcal{G} \), then
<|det|>equation [369, 436, 624, 467]<|/det|>\[
u = u - \left(\sum_ {x} m _ {x}\right) 1 = \sum_ {x} m _ {x} (x - 1).
\]
<|det|>text [201, 473, 555, 487]<|/det|>Thus, \(\mathcal{G}\) is generated by the nonzero \(x - 1\) for \(x\in G\).
<|det|>text [200, 488, 794, 534]<|/det|>Suppose that \(\sum_{x\neq 1}n_x(x - 1) = 0\). Then \(\sum_{x\neq 1}n_xx - (\sum_{x\neq 1}n_x)_1 = 0\) in \(\mathbb{Z}G\), which, as an abelian group, is free abelian with basis the elements of \(G\). Hence, \(n_x = 0\) for all \(x\neq 1\). Therefore, the nonzero \(x - 1\) comprise a basis of \(\mathcal{G}\).

<|det|>text [201, 566, 517, 581]<|/det|>Proposition 10.104. If \( A \) is a \( G \)-module, then
<|det|>equation [390, 588, 604, 603]<|/det|>\[
H _ {0} (G, A) = \mathbb {Z} \otimes_ {G} A \cong A / \mathcal {G} A.
\]
<|det|>text [200, 630, 794, 660]<|/det|>Proof. By definition, \( H_0(G, A) = \mathrm{Tor}_G^{\mathbb{Z}G}(\mathbb{Z}, A) = \mathbb{Z} \otimes_G A \). Applying the right exact functor \( \otimes_G A \) to the exact sequence
<|det|>equation [408, 667, 586, 681]<|/det|>\[
0 \to \mathcal {G} \to \mathbb {Z} G \to \mathbb {Z} \to 0
\]
<|det|>text [201, 688, 666, 704]<|/det|>gives exactness of the first row of the following commutative diagram:
<|det|>equation [329, 709, 667, 775]<|/det|>\[
\begin{array}{c} \mathcal {G} \otimes_ {G} A \longrightarrow \mathbb {Z} G \otimes_ {G} A \longrightarrow \mathbb {Z} \otimes_ {G} A \longrightarrow 0 \\ \Bigg \downarrow \\ \mathcal {G} A \longrightarrow A \longrightarrow A / \mathcal {G} A \longrightarrow 0 \end{array}
\]
<|det|>text [200, 781, 794, 811]<|/det|>The two solid vertical arrows are given by \( u \otimes a \mapsto ua \). By Proposition 8.93, there is an isomorphism \( \mathbb{Z} \otimes_G A \cong A / \mathcal{G}A \).

7.4 Linear Independence. Rank of a Matrix. Vector Space

Linear Independence and Dependence of Vectors

Given any set of m vectors  \( \mathbf{a}_{(1)}, \ldots, \mathbf{a}_{(m)} \)  (with the same number of components), a linear combination of these vectors is an expression of the form

\[
c _ {1} \mathbf {a} _ {(1) ^ {\prime}} + c _ {2} \mathbf {a} _ {(2) ^ {\prime}} + \dots + c _ {m} \mathbf {a} _ {(m)}
\]

where  \( c_{1} \) ,  \( c_{2} \) ,  \( \ldots \) ,  \( c_{m} \)  are any scalars.

Now consider the equation

\[
c _ {1} \mathbf {a} _ {(1) ^ {\prime}} + c _ {2} \mathbf {a} _ {(2) ^ {\prime}} + \dots + c _ {m} \mathbf {a} _ {(m)} = \mathbf {0} \tag {1}
\]

Clearly, this vector equation (1) holds if we choose all  \( c_{j} \) 's zero, because then it becomes 0 = 0.

If this is the only m-tuple of scalars for which (1) holds, then our vectors  \( \mathbf{a}_{(1)^{\prime}}, \ldots, \mathbf{a}_{(m)} \)  are said to form a linearly independent set or, more briefly, we call them linearly independent.

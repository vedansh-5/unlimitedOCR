<|det|>text [114, 142, 320, 159]<|/det|>This section is normative.
<|det|>text [111, 173, 856, 263]<|/det|>This section and the corresponding risk assessment guidance supplement the NIST Risk Management Framework [NIST RMF] and its component special publications. This does not establish additional risk management processes for agencies. Rather, requirements contained herein provide specific guidance related to digital identity risk that agency RPs SHALL apply while executing all relevant RMF lifecycle phases.
<|det|>title [113, 276, 248, 292]<|/det|>5.1 Overview
<|det|>text [111, 307, 881, 430]<|/det|>In today's digital services, combining proofing, authenticator, and federation requirements into a single bundle sometimes has unintended consequences and can put unnecessary implementation burden on the implementing organization. It is quite possible that an agency can deliver the most effective set of identity services by assessing the risk and impacts of failures for each individual component of digital authentication, rather than as a single, all-encompassing LOA. To this end, these guidelines recognize that an authentication error is not a singleton that drives all requirements.
<|det|>text [113, 445, 621, 464]<|/det|>This volume details requirements to assist agencies in avoiding:
<|det|>text [143, 479, 858, 514]<|/det|>1. Identity proofing errors (i.e., a false applicant claiming an identity that is not rightfully theirs);
<|det|>text [142, 515, 877, 547]<|/det|>2. Authentication errors (i.e., a false claimant using a credential that is not rightfully theirs); and
<|det|>text [142, 549, 655, 567]<|/det|>3. Federation errors (i.e., an identity assertion is compromised).
<|det|>text [112, 584, 825, 618]<|/det|>From the perspective of an identity proofing failure, there are two dimensions of potential failure:
<|det|>text [143, 636, 842, 671]<|/det|>1. The impact of providing a service to the wrong subject (e.g., an attacker successfully proofs as someone else).
<|det|>text [142, 672, 856, 707]<|/det|>2. The impact of excessive identity proofing (i.e., collecting and securely storing more information about a person than is required to successfully provide the digital service).
<|det|>text [112, 724, 831, 760]<|/det|>As such, agencies SHALL assess the risk of proofing, authentication, and federation errors separately to determine the required assurance level for each transaction.
<|det|>text [112, 774, 805, 810]<|/det|>Section 5.3 provides impact categories specific to digital identity to assist in the overall application of the RMF.
<|det|>text [111, 824, 879, 895]<|/det|>Risk assessments determine the extent to which risk must be mitigated by the identity proofing, authentication, and federation processes. These determinations drive the relevant choices of applicable technologies and mitigation strategies, rather than the desire for any given technology driving risk determinations.

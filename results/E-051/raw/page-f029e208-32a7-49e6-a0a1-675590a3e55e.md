<|det|>text [112, 489, 861, 529]<|/det|>Now we define a Jacobi-Koszul-Vinberg manifold as a symmetric analogue of a Jacobi manifold.
<|det|>text [112, 544, 861, 606]<|/det|>DEFINITION 2.9. A Jacobi-Koszul-Vinberg structure on an affine manifold \((M, \nabla)\) is a pair \((h, E)\) in \(\Gamma(S^2TM) \times \mathfrak{X}(M)\) satisfying, for any \(\alpha, \beta, \gamma\) in \(\Omega^1(M)\),
<|det|>text [217, 621, 767, 642]<|/det|>(i) \((\nabla_{h^{\sharp}\alpha}h)(\beta ,\gamma) - \langle \alpha ,E\rangle h(\beta ,\gamma) = (\nabla_{h^{\sharp}\beta}h)(\alpha ,\gamma) - \langle \beta ,E\rangle h(\alpha ,\gamma),\)
<|det|>text [214, 646, 625, 667]<|/det|>(ii) \((\nabla_{E}h)(\beta ,\gamma) - \langle \gamma ,\nabla_{h^{\sharp}\beta}E\rangle +\langle \beta ,E\rangle \langle \gamma ,E\rangle = 0,\)
<|det|>text [209, 673, 330, 692]<|/det|>(iii) \(\nabla_{E}E = 0\).
<|det|>text [114, 708, 586, 727]<|/det|>\((M,\nabla ,h,E)\) is called a Jacobi-Koszul-Vinberg manifold.
<|det|>text [112, 740, 862, 822]<|/det|>Let \((M, \nabla)\) be an affine manifold. As previously stated in Example 1.8, \((TM \oplus \mathbb{R}, \bar{\nabla}, \mathrm{pr}_1)\) is a left-symmetric algebroid over \(M\). Furthermore, because of Example 1.13 and Proposition 2.2, \(((TM \oplus \mathbb{R}, \bar{\nabla}, \mathrm{pr}_1), (0,1))\) is a Jacobi-left-symmetric algebroid over \(M\).
<|det|>text [112, 838, 862, 905]<|/det|>PROPOSITION 2.10. Let \((M, \nabla)\) be an affine manifold. Identify \((h, E)\) in \(\Gamma(S^2TM) \times \mathfrak{X}(M)\) with \(H\) in \(\Gamma(S^2(TM \oplus \mathbb{R}))\) by \(H = h + \frac{\partial}{\partial t} \otimes E + E \otimes \frac{\partial}{\partial t}\). Under this identification, the followings are equivalent;
<|det|>text [243, 920, 728, 939]<|/det|>(1) \((h,E)\) is a Jacobi-Koszul-Vinberg structure on \((M,\nabla)\)
<|det|>text [246, 943, 422, 966]<|/det|>(2) \(\llbracket H, H \rrbracket_{TM \oplus \mathbb{R}}^{(0,1)} = 0.\)

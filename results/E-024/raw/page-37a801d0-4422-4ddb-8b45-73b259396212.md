<|det|>title [111, 87, 398, 108]<|/det|>4 Numerical result
<|det|>text [109, 124, 889, 231]<|/det|>In this section, we undertake a numerical verification of the asymptotic normality for the initial Bayes estimator  \( \widehat{\mu}_{n,\varepsilon}^{(0)} \)  and the adaptive Bayes estimators  \( \widehat{\sigma}_{n,\varepsilon} \) ,  \( \widehat{\mu}_{n,\varepsilon} \)  and  \( \widehat{\alpha}_{\lambda} \)  in Theorem 3.1 of Section 3. Firstly, I will describe the setting of the simulation, including the algorithms for initial and adaptive Bayes estimators, as well as the execution environment. Additionally, I will address the crucial aspect of determining the threshold for jump estimation. Finally, I will conduct a discussion based on numerical result.
<|det|>text [138, 233, 541, 251]<|/det|>We consider Ornstein-Uhlenbeck (OU) process:
<|det|>equation [307, 261, 887, 283]<|/det|>\[
d X _ {t} = - \mu X _ {t} d t + \varepsilon \sigma d W _ {t} + \varepsilon d J _ {t} ^ {\lambda}, \quad X _ {0} ^ {\theta , \varepsilon} = 1. 0, \tag {4.1}
\]
<|det|>text [110, 297, 887, 355]<|/det|>where  \( J_{t}^{\lambda} = \sum_{k=1}^{N_{t}^{\lambda}} V_{\tau_{k}} \)  represents a compound Poisson process with an intensity of  \( \lambda = 100 \) . Let  \( \{V_{\tau_{k}}\}_{k=1,2,3,\ldots} \)  be a sequence of random variables with the inverse Gaussian distribution as in Example 3.2.3:
<|det|>equation [300, 365, 689, 416]<|/det|>\[
f _ {\alpha} (y) = \left\{ \begin{array}{l l} \sqrt {\frac {\alpha_ {2}}{\alpha \pi y ^ {2}}} \exp \left\{- \frac {\alpha_ {2} (y - \alpha_ {1}) ^ {2}}{2 \alpha_ {1} ^ {2} y} \right\} & (y > 0), \\ 0 & (y \leq 0). \end{array} \right.
\]
<|det|>text [110, 425, 888, 562]<|/det|>Let us denote the jump parameter \(\alpha := (\alpha_{1}, \alpha_{2})\). The parameter \(\theta = (\sigma, \mu, \alpha_{1}, \alpha_{2})\) remains unknown, with the true parameter set as \(\theta_{0} = (1.0, 2.0, 1.2, 0.5)\). Our parameter space is \(\Theta = [0.01, 50.0]^{4}\). Under combinations of \(n = 1000\), 2000, 5000 and \(\varepsilon = 0.1, 0.01\), we generated 1000 sample paths \(\{X_{t_k}^{\theta,\varepsilon}\}_{k=0}^n\) each following Equation (4.1). Subsequently, we calculated the initial Bayes estimator \(\widehat{\mu}_{n,\varepsilon}^{(0)}\) and the adaptive Bayes estimator \(\widehat{\theta}_{n,\varepsilon,\lambda} = (\widehat{\sigma}_{n,\varepsilon}, \widehat{\mu}_{n,\varepsilon}, \widehat{\alpha}_{1,\lambda}, \widehat{\alpha}_{2,\lambda})\). The means and standard deviations of the initial Bayes estimator \(\widehat{\mu}_{n,\varepsilon}^{(0)}\) and the adaptive Bayes estimator \(\widehat{\theta}_{n,\varepsilon,\lambda}\) are summarized in Table 1.
<|det|>text [110, 563, 887, 598]<|/det|>We computed the estimator using the MpCN method proposed by Kamatani [6]. The algorithm for the MpCN method is given as follows:
<|det|>text [139, 608, 551, 627]<|/det|>- Choose \( z \in \mathbb{R}^{d_i} \) for \( i = 0, 1, 2, 3 \) with \( d_{i_0} = d_{i_2} \).
<|det|>text [139, 637, 886, 674]<|/det|>- Generate \( \boldsymbol{z}_0 := \rho^{1/2} \boldsymbol{z} + (1 - \rho)^{1/2} \left( ||\boldsymbol{x}||^2 / ||\boldsymbol{w}_1||^2 \right) w_2 \), where \( w_1, w_2 \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I_{d_1}) \), \( (i = 0, 1, 2, 3) \).
<|det|>text [139, 686, 885, 723]<|/det|>- Accept \( \boldsymbol{z}_0 \) as \( \boldsymbol{z} \) with probability \( \min\{1, (p_i(\boldsymbol{z}_0)||\boldsymbol{z}||^2) / (p_i(\boldsymbol{z})||\boldsymbol{z}_0||^2)\} \) for \( i = 0, 1, 2, 3 \). Otherwise, discard \( \boldsymbol{z}_0 \).
<|det|>text [111, 734, 434, 752]<|/det|>Here, the target density \( p_i \) is given by
<|det|>equation [186, 763, 732, 808]<|/det|>\[
\rho_ {0} (\mu) := \left(\exp \{\Psi_ {0, \varepsilon} ^ {(0)} (\mu) \} \pi_ {2} (\mu)\right) \cdot \left(\int_ {\overline {{\Theta}} _ {2}} \exp \{\Psi_ {0, \varepsilon} ^ {(n)} (\mu) \} \pi_ {2} (\mu) d \mu\right) ^ {- 1},
\]
<|det|>equation [188, 810, 810, 854]<|/det|>\[
\rho_ {1} (\sigma) := \left(\exp \{\Psi_ {n, \varepsilon} ^ {(1)} (\sigma , \widehat {\mu} _ {n, \varepsilon} ^ {(0)}) \} \pi_ {1} (\sigma)\right) \cdot \left(\int_ {\overline {{\Theta}} _ {1}} \exp \{\Psi_ {n, \varepsilon} ^ {(1)} (\sigma , \widehat {\mu} _ {n, \varepsilon}) \} \pi_ {1} (\sigma) d \sigma\right) ^ {- 1},
\]
